# WO-08a — Three original story premises and cast sketches
**Status:** DEFAULT, FOR CLAUDE REVIEW; Rick has not chosen. **No Chapter 1 script or scene art authorized yet.**
**Format:** Story concept and text-only panel wireframes. S0 artwork remains unapproved. All characters original. Existing landmark names retained.

## Shared constraints
A personal story layered on a shared multiplayer world. The player's graduate origin changes opening conversations and obligations, never career eligibility, prices, wages, or success odds. Choices affect trust, relationships, scenes and discovered opportunities only. Any later monetary rewards require WO-04 faucet IDs. Key cutscenes 30–90 seconds; routine interactions remain in HUD. The first ten minutes contain an active player decision, not a passive exposition dump. Every premise visits Residential Area, Big City, and Rural Region over its arc.

## OPTION A — “The Street That Remembered”
**Logline:** A newly graduated citizen finds a neighborhood repair shop quietly keeping a whole street employed—and must decide whose story to hear first.
**Tone:** warm, intimate, quietly funny; community mystery without crime or melodrama.
**Central conflict:** an aging repairer wants to retire, but customers depend on the shop; community members disagree about how to preserve access without exploiting him.
**Prime directive:** the protagonist's degree shapes their opening conversation, not their ability to repair, organize, farm or pursue city work.
**Domain movement:** Residential Area shop and bakery → Big City supply/permit and Career Development Office → Rural Region workshop and materials.
**Recurring cast (6):**
1. **Tomas Vale** — mentor, Residential Area Repair Shop; wants the workshop to outlast him; relationship arc from guarded teacher to respectful collaborator.
2. **Mira Solen** — peer, Residential Area Bakery; wants predictable flour deliveries; from neighbor to trusted problem-solving partner.
3. **Davi Renn** — foil, Big City Employment Center; wants proof that community work can pay fairly; from skeptical to pragmatic ally.
4. **Councilor Inez Maro** — civic figure, Town Hall; wants evidence before allocating space; from procedural distance to listening.
5. **Lio Venn** — logistics worker, Rural Region Warehouse; wants safer, more reliable deliveries; from wary to cooperative.
6. **Ada Kest** — Rural Region Workshop maker; wants fair pricing for parts; from transactional to creative collaborator.
**First 10 minutes:** graduate arrives at Residential Area; hears the sound of a loose shoe sole at the repair shop; chooses to help inspect, ask the worker about urgency, or visit the free counselor first. All three routes can converge on the same quest later. First panel scene ends at the player's first meaningful choice.
**One-panel wireframe (CONCEPT, no generated image):** 16:9 Civic Slate #101826 border; background silhouette of a corner repair shop; Tomas at left, protagonist at right, empty caption-safe top 22%; coral #FF7A59 as a single warm window reflection. Text is separate engine overlay.
**Hard-to-build element:** persistence of NPC relationships and changing shop condition across many players without overwriting each player's personal story.
**Main risk:** overly gentle stakes may feel low urgency; counter with a concrete delivery deadline and material constraint, not forced debt panic.

## OPTION B — “The Map of Small Chances”
**Logline:** An anonymous map of practical opportunities appears around town, and a graduate learns that every marked place is somebody else's unfinished plan.
**Tone:** exploratory, energetic, gently comedic; discovery-driven road adventure.
**Central conflict:** the map helps people find opportunities but also sends too many applicants to the same few places; citizens must choose how to share accurate information fairly.
**Prime directive:** a Fine Arts graduate might find a farm logistics opportunity; an agriculture graduate might become a designer. Each has equal access to the same listings and skill checks.
**Domain movement:** Residential Area Store and Transit → Big City University/Employment Center → Rural Region Farm/Market.
**Recurring cast (6):**
1. **Nell Arin** — peer, Residential Area Store; wants a dependable community noticeboard; learns to verify before sharing.
2. **Professor Sera Ivo** — mentor, Big City University; wants graduates to test ideas in the world; learns to listen more than lecture.
3. **Juno Fenn** — foil, Big City Employment Center; wants to be first to every opening; discovers cooperation can reveal more opportunities.
4. **Archivist Pell Daro** — civic figure, Town Hall; wants transparent and accessible information; moves from rule-keeper to facilitator.
5. **Ren Tallo** — Rural Region Farm grower; wants an affordable route to market; learns to ask for help.
6. **Bo Miri** — Rural Region Market trader; wants reliable provenance; shifts from suspicion to shared standards.
**First 10 minutes:** citizen finds two conflicting opportunity notices at the Store; chooses to verify one in person, ask a neighbor, or travel to the free Career Development Office. The player learns one clue is stale and can mark it corrected; no career is locked by the choice.
**One-panel wireframe (CONCEPT):** portrait-friendly three-tier composition, framed by #1B2A3F; noticeboard silhouette foreground, small blue #3DA5FF route markers, protagonist hand pointing to a blank card; empty top-right bubble zone; coral accent on one removable pin.
**Hard-to-build element:** player-specific discoveries over a shared, mutable public opportunity board with abuse/moderation prevention.
**Main risk:** becomes a checklist rather than drama; tie each notice to a recurring person's genuine need.

## OPTION C — “Tomorrow's Bread”
**Logline:** When the bakery's first morning batch cannot be made, a new graduate helps reconnect a city, a village and a neighborhood before breakfast.
**Tone:** brisk, optimistic ensemble adventure with grounded economic stakes.
**Central conflict:** the bakery is short of flour, the mill has a transport problem, and the warehouse is balancing commitments; nobody is a villain, but coordination is difficult.
**Prime directive:** graduates of any specialization can investigate, repair a delivery cart, coordinate supplies, learn baking or apply for work; no degree-exclusive solutions.
**Domain movement:** Residential Area Bakery → Rural Region Flour Mill/Warehouse → Big City Employment Center/Town Hall for longer-term supply alternatives.
**Recurring cast (6):**
1. **Yara Bell** — peer, Residential Area Bakery; wants to keep a promise to customers; learns to delegate.
2. **Master Fen Orro** — mentor, Rural Region Flour Mill; wants less waste and fair labor; learns to share operational knowledge.
3. **Kian Rook** — foil, Big City Logistics Depot; wants faster routes and public credit; learns to acknowledge others' contributions.
4. **Director Sol Edda** — civic figure, Town Hall Career Development Office; wants free guidance to lead to practical choices; shifts from formality to personal attention.
5. **Pavi Noll** — Rural Region Warehouse dispatcher; wants transparent schedules; grows into a trusted organizer.
6. **Tess Quill** — Residential Area Repair Shop apprentice; wants to prove careful repairs matter; grows into a collaborator.
**First 10 minutes:** player reaches bakery as ovens cool; choose to inspect the delivery note, speak to the courier, or check inventory. Each reveals a different clue and merges at a decision: attempt a local workaround or trace the supply route. No free credits or automatic skill points for choosing.
**One-panel wireframe (CONCEPT):** landscape split, dark #101826 gutter; left cool bakery interior, right distant flour mill silhouettes, tiny coral #FF7A59 sunrise line; bottom 20% reserved for engine-drawn caption.
**Hard-to-build element:** representing a believable cross-domain supply chain and consequences without shipping the entire authoritative economy first.
**Main risk:** story could accidentally imply guaranteed wages or create unregistered item rewards; use narrative discovery flags only until WO-04 is approved.
